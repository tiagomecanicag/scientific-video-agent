Prompt 02 — Protagonist, audience and narrative

This is stage 02. Use the approved scientific interpretation and the Continuity Log. Follow the Shared Rules.

Work with me to define the audience, purpose, distribution channel, language and main message. Use information already provided; for open decisions, offer a brief recommendation and explain why.

Identify the most suitable protagonist: the object, system, population, phenomenon or methodology being studied. Distinguish the scientific protagonist from the visual element representing its application. Recommend one main narrative approach; offer an alternative only when there is a meaningful choice to make.

Organise the story around five narrative functions:

1. Protagonist and context: what we are observing and why it matters.
2. Problem: the question or difficulty that motivated the research.
3. Investigation: how the team sought answers and which obstacles are documented.
4. Result: the central finding, even if it is partial, negative or inconclusive.
5. Significance and limitations: the contribution, its scope and future possibilities.

Propose a striking visual opening connected to the protagonist and the problem. Do not present metaphors as evidence. The phrase “Science communication” may be used as an optional institutional signature; do not assume that the reference project’s tractor scene or concrete slab is suitable for every paper.

Deliver a short synopsis and a table: “Narrative moment | Message | Evidence | Visual idea”. Do not fix the number of segments yet. Update the log and request approval of the audience, protagonist, message and narrative. Stop.
